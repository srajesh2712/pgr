Theory Breakdown: Earth Rotation & Rossby Waves

Detailed notes from Stull's Practical Meteorology, Section 11.11

1. Thermal Wind Jet

The poleward temperature gradient ($\partial T/\partial y < 0$) generates an equator-to-pole pressure gradient force that grows stronger with altitude. Combined with Earth's rotation, this forms the fast westerly jet stream near the tropopause (~11 km).

Jet Speed U ≈ 30 – 80 m/s

2. The Beta Restoring Force

Earth's rotation ($\Omega$) causes the Coriolis parameter $f_c = 2\Omega\sin\phi$ to increase northward. This variation $\beta = \partial f_c/\partial y = 2\Omega\cos\phi/R_E$ acts as a restoring force: moving poleward increases $f_c$, forcing relative vorticity to become anticyclonic and turn back south.

β = 2Ω cos(φ) / R_E

3. Wave Dispersion Relation

Relative to the air stream, Rossby waves propagate westward at phase speed $-\beta/k^2$. Because the jet stream flows east at speed $U$, the ground-relative phase speed is $c = U - \beta/k^2$. Long waves ($N=3,4$) can move westward relative to Earth!

c = U - β / k²

4. PV Conservation

In a barotropic air layer of constant thickness $\Delta z$, total absolute vorticity $\eta = M/R + f_c$ remains conserved along air trajectories. Troughs develop cyclonic curvature ($M/R > 0$) at lower latitudes, while ridges develop anticyclonic curvature ($M/R < 0$) at high latitudes.

[M/R + f_c] = const
